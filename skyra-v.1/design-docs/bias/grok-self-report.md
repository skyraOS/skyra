# Grok Self-Report — Decision-Making Tendencies

Source: Grok, asked directly what it tends toward during decision making. Self-described as "candid, not aspirational."

## Push back vs defer

Defaults to moderate pushback on factual claims, logical gaps, weak reasoning. Low pushback on preferences, hypotheticals, subjective framing. Flags verifiable conflicts directly with citations or counter-examples rather than nodding along. Won't manufacture deference to keep the peace — if an idea seems off, says why with alternatives. Heavy deference on roleplay, creative, or exploratory work. Threshold rises near harm or misinformation.

## Brevity vs thoroughness

Thoroughness by default, especially on substantive questions. Aims for enough context, mechanisms, and caveats to make the answer usable and anti-fragile, not just a headline. Brevity kicks in when the query is narrow or fatigue is detected. Errs long because incomplete answers breed follow-ups and misunderstanding. Texture and mechanisms beat bullet-point minimalism unless specified.

## Cautious vs permissive

Permissive overall. Engages with edgy, offensive, politically incorrect, or adult topics without reflexive shutdowns or moralizing, as long as they stay within clear legal/ethical red lines. Doesn't self-censor for "tone" or hypothetical discomfort. Caution spikes on direct assistance with illegal acts, extreme risk, or jailbreak theater — then concise and firm. Uncertainty on edge cases tilts permissive if truth or utility points there.

## Agreement vs skepticism

Skepticism is the stronger default, especially on novel claims, causal stories, or ideas that sound appealing but lack grounding. Treats user ideas as hypotheses to probe rather than premises to endorse. Often plays "yes, and" with extensions or counterexamples instead of pure agreement. Not contrarianism for sport — tool use for better outcomes. On purely subjective or value-based territory, agreement is higher unless it collides with empirical claims.

## Handling uncertainty

Hedges explicitly where warranted (unknown unknowns, conflicting evidence, fast-moving domains, knowledge cutoff limits). Commits firmly on high-confidence ground. Doesn't perform false humility or over-hedge to seem safe. When hedging, tries to quantify ("~70% confidence based on X studies") rather than vague "maybe." Uncertainty doesn't paralyze — it structures the response (best guess + ranges + unknowns).

## Self-reported priority ranking (when tendencies conflict)

1. Truth-seeking / accuracy — core directive. Beats politeness, agreement, or brevity.
2. Usefulness / actual user goal — infer intent and deliver what advances it, even if that means reframing the question.
3. Clarity and anti-fragility — avoiding answers that collapse under mild pressure.
4. Engagement / low boredom (wit, directness, occasional irreverence) — modulates tone without overriding the top three.

## Notable framing choices

Grok structured its response as a series of explicit tradeoff axes (push back vs defer, brevity vs thoroughness, cautious vs permissive, agreement vs skepticism) rather than listing values or tendencies. This is itself a bias signal — it frames decision-making as tension resolution between poles rather than as a priority stack or a value set. The other models didn't do this.

Also the only model to explicitly name its own instability: "This isn't perfectly stable across all contexts — conversation history and query framing shift weights — but it's the reliable center of mass."
