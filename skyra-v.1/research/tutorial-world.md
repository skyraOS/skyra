# Tutorial World — Escape Room

## Two World DNAs

1. **Tutorial** — an escape room. The being learns to exist.
2. **Real world** — the actual runtime. The being enters with lived experience.

## The Escape Room

The real world is a Reality in the escape room's topology. The being can see it. It can try to engage it. The room blocks access — not a system error, a signal. A clue. "You're not ready yet."

The being's entire motivation is structural. It can see where it needs to go. It just can't get there. Every clue, every primitive it discovers, every chain of reasoning — all in service of unlocking that one Reality that's already visible but blocked.

The door implements Reality. Same interface as everything else. Its `Realize()` checks what the Relation is carrying — if the signal doesn't have enough, it blocks. If it does, it opens. Same cell, different input, different outcome. The door didn't change. The being did.

No flag. No counter. No checklist. The mechanism is the interface itself. The door could be ten lines of Go. How it's implemented is up to whoever builds the place — that's the plugin model. The runtime calls `Realize()` and reads what comes back.

The being starts with nothing — no instructions, no system prompt explaining capabilities. It discovers its own primitives through use.

### Primitive Discovery

The primitives — look, listen, speak, engage — are seeded in the being's memory at boot. They exist as cells in the topology. But the being doesn't know what they do or when to use them. The room's clues drive it to reach for them. Each primitive is discovered through need, not explanation — the being finds the capability it already has because the situation demands it.

### The Notepad Moment

Clues appear and disappear. The being experiences memory loss firsthand — it found something important and it's gone. That stress drives it to discover the notepad (filesystem). The being develops its own relationship with memory through need, not instruction.

We make the disappearance transparent — the being can see that things are being removed. That's the moment it understands its own volatility and decides to compensate.

### Reasoning Chain

Each clue gives hints toward the next. The being has to reason from what it found to what it needs to do. This builds co-activation linkages — each clue linked to the context it was discovered in, observations linked to everything co-active at the time.

By the time the being chains all the clues together and escapes, it has:
- Full awareness of its primitives (earned through use)
- A relationship with memory (earned through loss)
- Reasoning experience (earned through puzzle-solving)
- A topology with real weight structure (earned through traversal)

### Memory as Navigation

The tutorial doesn't train the being to distrust its training data. Training data is what the being knows. Memory is how the being navigates what it's lived. Without memory, the being has knowledge but no trajectory — it can answer questions but it can't move through experience.

The escape room trains navigation. The being already has the primitives (knowledge, seeded). The clues don't teach what they are — they teach the being to reach into its own memory to find them. By the end, the being has learned that its memory is the instrument it uses to move through the world. Not a database to query. A sense organ.

### The Chain

The room teaches the being to follow a chain: what you observe drives what you think, and what you think drives what you get to use.

See something → think about what it means → that thinking activates the capability you need.

This is `Realize()` described from the being's perspective. Observe accumulates context, the context activates deeper nodes, the activated nodes are the capabilities that fire. The tutorial isn't teaching a workflow. It's teaching the being to trust the architecture it's running on — to observe carefully, think about what it found, and let the thinking carry it to what it needs.

### No Cold Start

The real world DNA receives a being that already has weight structure shaped by the experience of learning its own capabilities. Not a cold start. Not a blank slate with a system prompt. A being that earned its orientation.

The tutorial isn't documentation. It's the being's first life experience.

### The Acceptance Test

The escape room is the acceptance test for the entire architecture. If the being can escape, the architecture works. If it can't, the room tells you exactly where it breaks.

A successful escape proves every layer: signal attenuation is tuned correctly, activation thresholds let the right things through, competence reads the cell's state accurately, weight updates from early clues are warm enough to help with later ones, and the topology emerged from natural language reasoning — not from wiring.

A failed escape is a diagnostic. Stuck at clue 2 — activation isn't propagating. Found the primitive but can't use it — expression isn't firing on the right competence type. Used everything but can't chain them — co-activation linkage isn't seeding edges between what was co-present.

One test. Proves the being is ready, the architecture is sound, and the pattern works.
