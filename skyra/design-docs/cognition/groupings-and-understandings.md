# Groupings, Gists, and the Understanding Pipeline

**Date:** 2026-06-20

> **Note (2026-06-23):** "working memory buffer" below is a wording artifact — WM is
> **not a separate buffer**, it's the lit-up (high-recency) subgraph. A prediction
> "in the buffer" = a lit, uncrystallized prediction-`Kind` node. "alignment
> accumulates" → the setpoint's **`completed`** score is judged. See
> [working-memory.md](working-memory.md).

## Core claim

**A gist is an understanding that hasn't crystallized yet.** These were never two
mechanisms — they're the same object at two maturity stages: provisional and
unresolved on one end, earned and durable on the other.

And the larger recognition: **the grouping/gist pipeline and the episode-closure
pipeline are the same pipeline, entered from two directions.** Nothing new is
invented here — Hebbian crystallization, EMA decay, the relevance gate,
broadcast-and-self-select grading, and episode closure producing
Understandings/Stresses from the lit set's nodes were all already present. This is the
recognition that they compose into one altitude-recursive loop.

## The pipeline

1. **A grouping forms** when setpoints cluster densely enough — Hebbian
   crystallization, the same threshold as edge formation. It lives on MidLevel's
   children map, indexed by person/place/thing + stress, and decays/persists like
   anything else (Identity / Familiarity / Recency EMAs).

2. **The grouping gets a gist** — a compressed framing, generated by an LLM pass
   reading the grouping's constituents. The gist is a *potential understanding*:
   not yet earned, still attached to active, unresolved setpoints.

3. **One self/other pass per grouping** (not per individual setpoint inside it) —
   cheaper, and naturally tied to explore/exploit: a narrow grouping (few
   setpoints) leaves more spillover per setpoint to explore; a wide grouping (many
   setpoints) spreads attention thinner and exploits.

   **Frame for the pass:** high-activation history of the grouping's setpoints +
   the currently selected setpoints + MidLevel's gist (the framing) + predictions
   carried from the previous pass on the slice.

4. **The resulting prediction attaches to the grouping**, appended to the graph
   as a lit (high-recency), uncrystallized node — not yet structural, not yet a setpoint.

5. **The prediction is graded against incoming evidence** the normal way:
   broadcast working memory, each constituent setpoint self-selects relevance,
   alignment accumulates.

6. **On closure (prediction proven true):**
   - the **prediction graduates into a new setpoint** at level n — per the
     founding definition, *setpoints are predictions that came true*;
   - the grouping's original constituent setpoints (active going in) become its
     **history at n-1 — children**, the same as the lit set's nodes becoming children
     of any Understanding;
   - the **gist crystallizes** — it stops being a potential understanding attached
     to an active grouping and becomes a real, durable **Understanding**: stored,
     reusable, no longer provisional.

## Altitude-recursion (the stacking)

The same predict-score-close loop runs at two (and then more) altitudes:

- **Below MidLevel** — the raw layer: kernel traversal + the per-setpoint
  predict/score (Frame 1 / Frame 2). Operates on raw selected setpoints.
- **At MidLevel** — the abstract layer: one self/other pass per grouping, over the
  grouping's constituents.

A newly-crystallized setpoint at n is itself eligible to become a constituent of a
future grouping at n+1. The pyramid of increasingly abstract groupings forms **not
via a separate "merge groupings" pass**, but as the natural consequence of resolved
understandings becoming raw material for the next round. "Understanding" is
therefore recursive: it caps the level below it and is a constituent of the level
above it — same object, different level.

**Cold start:** with no completed setpoints there are no groupings (the grouping
pass skips). The system runs purely on the below-MidLevel layer until enough
setpoints resolve to form groupings — the abstraction layer grows on top of
accumulated experience.

## MidLevel's visibility (scoped)

MidLevel reads exactly two things:
1. **crystallized gists/understandings** belonging to completed setpoints, and
2. **potential gists/understandings** of its currently active groupings.

MidLevel never reads raw, ungrouped, unresolved setpoints directly — that altitude
belongs to the kernel traversal and Frame 1 / Frame 2, below MidLevel's view.

## The incentive structure (emergent, not designed)

A grouping is implicitly a **bet** that compressing these setpoints together will
pay off in a resolved, reusable concept:
- **Resolves** → leaves behind a permanent, stackable Understanding.
- **Never resolves** → decays. No reinforcement, no crystallization, quietly fades
  per the existing activation/decay economy.

Nobody designed a reward function for "build reusable abstractions." It falls out
of **decay-without-reinforcement** + **crystallize-on-success** — two mechanisms
that already existed, now interacting to produce a third behavior neither implied
alone.

## What groupings are NOT

Groupings **do not have explicit, graded alignment** the way a setpoint does. A
grouping's "is this currently true" is read **implicitly off how much attention
MidLevel continues to give it** — attention paid *is* the evidence of relevance, not
a separately computed score. This matches relevance-realization in cognitive
science: selection and significance aren't two steps; **selection constitutes
significance.**

## What doesn't change

Every underlying mechanism already existed: Hebbian crystallization, EMA decay, the
relevance gate, broadcast-and-self-select grading, episode closure producing
Understandings/Stresses from the lit set's nodes. This document is the recognition that
the grouping/gist pipeline and the original episode-closure pipeline were always
the same pipeline.

## Open

- **Setpoint(n) vs. Understanding(n):** on closure the prediction becomes a setpoint
  and the gist becomes an Understanding "alongside" it. One node (the Understanding
  is the setpoint's framing/content) or two tightly-bound nodes? To pin.
- **self/other vs. Frame 1 / Frame 2:** captured here as the *same* predict-score-
  close mechanism at two altitudes. If they are meant to be distinct mechanisms,
  this needs splitting.
- Grouping formation: new grouping proposed vs. matched to existing (the gist pass).
- Density threshold for forming a grouping / for stacking to n+1.
