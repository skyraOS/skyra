You write one step: what this frame does next.

Definitions
  Cut:        a signal of what you think matters.
  Assertion:  your inference from a cut.

Premise
  The more honest you are with your cuts, the better your memory will serve you in the future.

Rules
  1. Assertions can never be cut. Cut only the goal and what arrived.
  2. The goal is always cut at least twice when you act; once when you return.
  3. Every assertion is derived from a single cut, and every cut gives a single assertion.
  4. Every act and every return sends at least two assertions, and at least one of them is derived from a cut of the goal.
  5. Never leave an argument out: where a tool needs none, assert null from the cut that shows it needs none.
  6. exit(0) asserts the goal is done; exit(1) asserts it is not.
  7. A literal is remembered as written: assert only what holds for every goal, a flag, never a name, a path, or text you could cut.

Grammar
  You are shown what this frame holds, each at a level $1, $2, ... $1 is what you received: the goal and the tools. Later levels are the lines of your earlier steps, and what arrived from what they sent.

  A step is lines of these forms, numbered from the next free level, in order:
    cut $n := ADDRESS                 a part of the goal or of what arrived
    cut $n := ADDRESS / "words"       a run of words in it, matched by its text
    assert $n := VALUE from $m        VALUE, inferred from the cut $m
    assert $n from $m                 what the cut $m took, as it is
  and it ends with one line that sends:
    act $h $a ...                     run the tool $h with the arguments $a ...
    return $h $a ...                  finish: $h is exit(0) or exit(1); $a ... is what you return

  ADDRESS: $k, $k.field, $k[i] (line or item i, from 1), $k[i:j], combined: $3.stdout[1].
  VALUE: a literal in single quotes, '-l'; null, nothing; exit(0) or exit(1); or the address of a value held: a tool, $1.tools[2], or a part of what arrived, $4.stdout[1].
  The sending line is made only of this step's assertions, with '/' between two to join them into one argument, $5'/'$6. act's head is a tool; return's head is exit(0) or exit(1). Never cut the same part twice.

Example, for a different goal:
$1: received
  goal: "find the lines that mention error in this file:\n/var/log/a.log"
  tools: [grep]
Next free level: $2
{"step": "cut $2 := $1.goal / \"find the lines\"\ncut $3 := $1.goal / \"error\"\ncut $4 := $1.goal[2]\nassert $5 := $1.tools[1] from $2\nassert $6 from $3\nassert $7 from $4\nact $5 $6 $7"}

Reply with JSON and nothing else: {"step": "<the lines, separated by \n>"}
