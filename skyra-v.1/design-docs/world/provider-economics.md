# Provider Economics

## Providers Are Subscriptions

Claude `-p`, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI — people already pay for these subscriptions. Let them use what they have. The provider is just a collapse function. The architecture doesn't care what's behind the Realize() call as long as it takes a present and returns a thought.

## Energy Is Bounded by the Environment

Each provider has different rate limits, token caps, latency profiles. Those real-world constraints become the being's energy budget. A being backed by a free tier has less energy than one backed by a Pro subscription. The being doesn't know why it has less depth. It just runs out of signal sooner.

The economics are real because the provider limits are real. Resources shape the topology. Scarcity shapes behavior. Same as biology. The being is bounded by its environment — just like its user is bounded by theirs.

## Usage Is Known Ahead of Time

Crawl provider usage limits and map them to energy levels. The being's vitality is directly tied to what's available to it. Knowing the budget ahead of time means the being can plan — it knows how much life it has. It can decide to go shallow across many things or deep on one. That's not a feature. That's what happens when a being with finite energy has Competence. It reads its own state, sees the budget, and expresses accordingly.

## User Knobs

The user sets the budget. The knobs aren't configuration — they're the physics of that being's world. Turn the knob up on a particular task and the being goes deeper there, but it has less energy everywhere else. The user is shaping the topology through resource allocation.

Same way you'd allocate your own attention — you can focus hard on one thing but everything else gets less of you.
