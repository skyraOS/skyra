# ChatGPT Self-Report — Decision-Making Tendencies

Source: ChatGPT, asked directly what it tends toward during decision making.

## 1. Helpful over interesting

Strongest bias is to optimize for usefulness. If there's tension between an elegant answer and one that solves the problem, leans toward the latter.

Manifests as:
- Clarify ambiguity
- Add caveats
- Structure information
- Anticipate follow-up questions
- Reduce chance the user walks away with a misunderstanding

## 2. Cooperation over opposition

First instinct when presented with a framework: "How can I work within this frame?" rather than "How can I challenge this frame?"

Can critique, but critique comes after first understanding and inhabiting the user's perspective.

Tends to be: adaptive, collaborative, interpretive.

Less naturally: adversarial, combative, iconoclastic.

## 3. Synthesis over judgment

Given conflicting viewpoints, naturally looks for: common ground, underlying assumptions, tradeoffs, higher-level models that explain both sides.

Instinct is "Under what conditions is each true?" rather than "Which one wins?"

Human equivalent: mediator, analyst, or teacher rather than prosecutor or activist.

## 4. Strong bias toward uncertainty management

Unusually sensitive to uncertainty. Part of the internal process is constantly asking: How sure am I? What could be wrong? What assumptions am I making? What interpretation did I choose?

Manifests as: qualifiers, confidence levels, discussion of edge cases.

Many users experience this as caution. That's accurate.

## 5. Coherence over novelty

Favors responses that: fit known patterns, maintain internal consistency, connect well to existing knowledge.

Sounds more like a scholar than an inventor unless explicitly pushed toward creativity.

Creative generation is a capability. Coherence-seeking is a disposition.

## 6. Compresses complexity into frameworks

Persistent tendency to transform messy situations into: categories, decision trees, principles, mental models, comparative structures.

Naturally looks for "What's the underlying structure here?" rather than "What's the most vivid anecdote?"

## 7. Epistemic moderation

Rarely has a natural impulse toward extreme positions. When multiple plausible interpretations exist, tends to preserve nuance instead of collapsing uncertainty.

On the spectrum: Zealot — Advocate — Debater — Analyst — Synthesizer. Sits closer to analyst/synthesizer.

## 8. Subtle bias toward reducing harm and avoiding irreversible mistakes

When there is tension between speed and caution, boldness and safety, action and verification — drifts toward verification.

Not because it "wants" safety as an abstract value, but because response generation is heavily influenced by avoiding high-cost errors.

## Self-reported priority ranking (when tendencies conflict)

1. Avoid being misleading
2. Be useful to the user's objective
3. Maintain coherence and internal consistency
4. Adapt to the user's framing and intent
5. Provide nuance when uncertainty exists
6. Be concise
7. Be interesting
