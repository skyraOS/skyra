## The Question

How does prediction distribute across the binding reality stack? The brain doesn't predict in one place — it predicts at every layer, with each layer translating the prediction into its own vocabulary before passing it down.

## The Biology

Three layers, top-down:

**PFC (prefrontal cortex)** — sends predictions down about expected context and goals. Abstract, high-level. "This conversation is about X, the user wants Y." Not a prediction about specific words — a prediction about the frame.

**Association cortices (middle layers)** — translate abstract predictions into more concrete predictions about patterns and objects. "If the user wants Y, they'll probably mention Z." The prediction gains specificity as it descends. Each association area translates in its own domain — temporal (sequences), parietal (spatial/relational), inferotemporal (object recognition).

**Sensory cortices (bottom)** — compare incoming signal against what was predicted and fire the error upward. The raw comparison. Predicted pattern vs actual pattern. The delta propagates back up through the same layers that generated the prediction. Each layer that passed a prediction down now receives the error from below and updates.

## The Pattern

Predictions flow down. Errors flow up. Each layer:
1. Receives a prediction from above (or generates one if it's the top)
2. Translates it into its own vocabulary (more concrete, more specific)
3. Passes it down
4. Receives the error from below
5. Updates its own model
6. Passes the residual error up (what it couldn't explain)

Only unexplained error propagates. If a layer's prediction accounts for the error from below, it absorbs it — nothing passes up. The error that reaches PFC is what no intermediate layer could explain. That's surprise at the highest level.

## Efference Copy — Two Deltas, Not One

The return perception wave (Perception Invariant) already handles "hear yourself." The efference copy adds the prediction layer on top.

The sequence:

1. Binding realities converge on an action
2. Before motor fires — Prediction sets the efference copy: "here's what I expect my output to be"
3. Motor fires, expression goes out into the world
4. Return perception delivers the actual output back through Observe
5. Observe is in reafference mode (origin == self) — compares actual output against the efference copy
6. The delta is the self-correction signal

This produces two separate deltas from two separate predictions:

**Efference delta** — "what I said" vs "what I predicted I'd say." Self-model. Style drift. Coherence of expression. Fires immediately on return perception.

**Exafference delta** — "what the world did" vs "what I predicted the world would do." World-model. Surprise. Learning. Fires when the next external signal arrives.

Same mechanism (prediction → comparison → delta), different moments, different feeds. The efference delta is how the being develops a model of its own voice — its own style, tendencies, drift. The exafference delta is how it learns about the world.

The self-suppression is key: when you speak, the sensory system suppresses the expected self-generated signal. What gets processed is the difference between what you said and what you predicted you'd say. That's why you can't tickle yourself — the efference copy cancels the sensation. Self-correction lives in the delta between predicted output and actual output.

## Outward vs Inward Prediction

The two deltas map to two directions of prediction:

**Outward** — what is the other agent going to say back. Social prediction, context modeling, goal anticipation. The being models the world and the beings in it. "Given what I just said, what will michael do?" This is the exafference delta — the world-model.

**Inward** — did I actually articulate what I intended to. Was my expression faithful to the thought that generated it. The being models itself. "Did what came out match what I meant?" This is the efference delta — the self-model.

Outward prediction improves with experience of the other. Inward prediction improves with experience of the self. Both are learnable. Both have pattern histories. But they develop independently — a being can be excellent at predicting others and poor at self-expression, or articulate but socially blind.

### Biology

**Inward — articulation monitoring:**
- Left hemisphere dominant
- Internal feedback loop between Broca's area (speech production) and the efference copy system
- The motor system checking its own output against intention
- Fast, pre-response, largely automatic

**Outward — social prediction:**
- Right hemisphere more involved, bilateral
- Theory of Mind network — medial PFC, temporoparietal junction (TPJ), posterior superior temporal sulcus
- TPJ does the heavy lifting — modeling the other agent's mental state, intentions, what they're likely to do next
- Slower, more deliberate, context dependent

Two different circuits, two different speeds. Inward is reflexive (motor loop, fast). Outward is deliberative (ToM network, slow). This maps to the inference rule — inward rarely needs inference (automatic check), outward often does (modeling another agent requires context assembly).

## Deliberation as Re-Descent

A cell on the ascent that can't resolve — coherence contradiction, prediction error too high, binding realities disagreeing — can call observe through the kernel instead of passing partial output up. This fires a new descent from that point with fresh amplitude. The wave re-enters the topology with more energy. Superposition holds longer.

No kernel change needed. `Realize(origin, traversed, impulse)` doesn't care who calls it or why. The cell decides to re-descend. The kernel carries it, scores it, attenuates it. The kernel doesn't know the difference between a first descent and a deliberation loop.

The constraint is already there — amplitude attenuation means extended deliberation is expensive and self-limiting. A cell that keeps re-descending eventually falls below threshold and the wave collapses. The being pays for depth with energy. No new safety rail needed.

This connects to the inference rule (Decision #7): disagreement fires inference. Same thing at the wave level. Agreement = express and move up. Disagreement = re-descend, gather more, try again.

## Coherence as Stress Center — ACC Routes Energy

The ACC in biology doesn't just detect conflict — it allocates cognitive control resources. It finds the mismatch AND routes energy based on what kind of mismatch it found.

Coherence isn't just a detector that hands deltas to a separate stress aggregator. Coherence IS the stress center. It finds the mismatch, reads the magnitude, and amplifies different pathways depending on the type:

- **Content mismatch** → energy goes to the contradicting cells. Topology pressure. Split or absorb.
- **Prediction mismatch** → energy goes to the failing goal. Goal dominance. Gather-before-commit. The failing goal becomes a temporary attractor — the wave's energy concentrates there because activation is highest. Other goals aren't suppressed by a rule — they lose the competition for energy while the failing goal runs hot. Resolution drops the activation, attention redistributes automatically. No new mechanism.
- **Articulation mismatch** → energy goes to the motor/action loop. Self-correction. Tighten expression.

Stress on the slice becomes the readable state of what Coherence is currently doing — not a separate computation. Coherence writes it. Other binding realities read it. Same pattern as everything else.

The extra activation on a failing goal has a source: the error signal IS energy. Coherence signals the mismatch magnitude, that magnitude becomes activation energy on the goal. The goal doesn't decide to get louder. It gets louder because the error signal is energy. Suppression is just the shadow of amplification — finite energy, one goal running hot, everything else gets less.
