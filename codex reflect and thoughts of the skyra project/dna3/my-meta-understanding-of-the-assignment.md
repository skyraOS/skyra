# My Meta-Understanding of the Assignment

This remains a controlled, longitudinal examination of Skyra and of the critic
examining Skyra.

Each version is presented within a restricted evidentiary boundary. I must judge
what its code supports before seeing what follows, then preserve that judgment so
later work cannot silently rewrite my earlier position.

The symbol system adds a second obligation. Criticisms must persist long enough
to test architectural progress, but the critic must not preserve them after the
evidence changes. For dnav3 I therefore had to distinguish:

- criticisms that still stand;
- criticisms the implementation materially answered;
- criticisms I formulated too broadly to remain valid.

The third category makes the observer part of the audit. A criticism can fail by
being reductive, by combining independent questions, or by refusing to recognize
that a later representation changed the terms of the problem.

The line constraints are also methodological. Reflection must not reproduce the
same failure it criticizes: conceptual complexity growing faster than useful
evidence. A mistake should be decomposed precisely and then compressed.

The `dnav3` record is now another fixed boundary. It says what progressed, what
remains unearned, and where my own critical vocabulary required correction before
the next version is revealed.

In a phrase:

> This is a versioned epistemic audit in which both the architecture and its
> observer must revise their claims when the evidence changes.
